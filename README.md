# Project 1: *Manuscript*

Demo/template for our [first projects](https://typography-interaction-2627.github.io/project/1/).

> **Students will choose a seminal design text from [readings.design](https://readings.design), read and respond to it, and typeset their selection and reply together.**
>
> The goal of this project is to hone your basic skills in typography, focusing on expression, hierarchy, and form appropriate to a work. You will do this through exploration, trial and error, and responding to critical feedback. And then you will execute this typesetting in code, as a web page

*About

		*The Reading
	Chimeros main point in the content of his original page is that screens are defined by their capacity to change. The main reason why I chose this reading was because the presentation of familiar or relatable descriptions to make abstract ideas easier to understand throughout the article made it very engaging and easy to digest.


		*My Response
	The article sparked the approach that I took to my design of the article which is how can I use familiar comparisons (seen in the image replacement and overall setup of the document) and intentional design choices (such as creating sections of the article) to help readers understand the ideas being communicated to them.

*Design Concept

	** Color
		The colors are intentionally chosen to have a modern direction on the old green-screen monitors. Traditionally they have a black background and very bright green blocky text, but my intention was to see how I could modernize it on a lighter background. The page uses green on th heads and highlights while maintaining a dark green as the main color of the text. Instead of going for the traditional bright green, I wanted the page to have its own identity while taking some points from history, while still communicating the authors main argument: "Screens are not defined by how thing looks but by their "grain",whcih is their capacity for change.

		Blue links intentionally kept blue to get the eye of the user for them to easily identify that those are links that I want them to reference so that they get a better understanding of the content.


	** Type
		Space Mono: Title, headings to communicate that traditional machine look and connection back to those old green-screen computers.

		Fraunces: The body tex communicating the human voice of both the author of the article and myself

		I switched from Roboto and Barlow as my original type faces after getting feedback that it felt to safe and getting the information that I did not know that it is closely related to Android as they used it for many years.


	**Reading and Annotations
		I divided the article into sections to make the long blocks of text feel shorter to users. The aim was to keep this very heavy article (not only in length but in content) as digestable as possible while still keeping users egagegd.

		Highlights are used to show defintions

		Underlines are used to bring attention to important sayings and points in the reading

		Bolding was used as a direct reflection and or following the styling and emphasis of the original article

		Links where implemented throughout the article as well to keep connections close and for users to go out in their own and have a resource to the defintions of things that are being referenced in the article while adhering to the no image requirements.


	** No Images
		The project guidline did not allow images so I gave it a different approach of replacing them with text, reference links, or relatable associations.


	** How I built it
		Design System with CSS variables for the colors, fonts, and general sizes

		Rem and ch for maximum line length

		Implemented the stick sections so that the readers know what section of the article they are in
